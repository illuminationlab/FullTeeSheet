How to fill empty tee times

How to Fill Empty Tee Times
Without Slashing Your Rates.

Empty midweek and twilight slots don't have to mean discounts or barter deals. Here's how to fill tee times from your own golfer database.

The fastest way to fill empty tee times without cutting your rates is to work the list you already have. Your own golfers — the ones who've played before, given you a phone number, and shown you when they like to tee off — are a warmer audience than any discount deal site will ever send you. Segment that database, reach the right golfers at the right moment with a text and email, and backfill cancellations automatically, and you fill soft blocks without ever touching your price sheet. Here's the play, step by step.

1. Why your slow blocks are slow.

Almost every course shares the same shape of demand. Weekend mornings sell themselves. Then Tuesday through Thursday mornings sit soft, and the twilight window after the after-work rush thins out fast. It isn't a demand problem so much as a timing problem: the golfers who would play those slots aren't thinking about golf at 9am on a Wednesday, and nothing reminds them.

The instinct is to treat a soft block as a pricing problem and reach for a discount. But the block isn't empty because your rate is too high. It's empty because the right golfer didn't get a nudge at the right moment. Fix the nudge and you keep the rate.

2. The hidden cost of barter and blanket discounts.

When a slow Tuesday panics you into a blanket 40%-off blast or a barter deal that trades tee times for "free" software or advertising, two things happen. First, you train your regulars to wait for the discount — why book Saturday at rack rate when a coupon is coming Wednesday? Second, you hand your pricing control to someone else. Barter especially isn't free: you're paying in inventory, and every bartered round is a round you can't sell.

Discounting also fills the slot with your least loyal, most price-sensitive golfer, when the goal is to fill it with a regular who'll spend in the grill and come back at full rate. Protecting your price sheet is the whole point — and it's exactly what FullTeeSheet's automation tools are built to do without giving margin away.

3. Fill gaps from your own database.

Your booking history is a segmentation goldmine most courses never open. Every golfer who's played leaves a trail: when they tee off, how often, whether they walk or ride, whether they came for a league or a one-off. Group them and the soft-block problem starts to solve itself:

The midweek retiree. Flexible schedule, loves an empty course, plays Tuesday and Thursday mornings if reminded. This is your slow-morning audience.

The after-work twilight player. Younger, plays nine after five, price-aware but time-rich in the evenings. This is your twilight audience.

The lapsed regular. Played monthly last season, hasn't been back in eight weeks. One well-timed "we miss you" text brings a chunk of them back.

Instead of blasting your whole list, you send a targeted note to the segment that actually plays that block. It reads as a helpful heads-up, not a fire sale — and it fills the slot at your normal rate.

4. Automated waitlists and cancellation fill.

The other side of a full tee sheet is what happens when someone drops. A Saturday cancellation at 7am is a hole in your best inventory — and if nobody refills it in time, that's a round gone. An automated waitlist turns every cancellation back into a booked round: the moment a slot frees up, the system texts the golfers who wanted that time window, and the first to tap "book" gets it. No front-counter phone tag, no slot sitting open until someone happens to call.

This is where a no-show quietly becomes a reclaimed round. Pair the waitlist with reminders and the two systems feed each other — the reminder frees the slot early enough that the waitlist can actually resell it.

5. Set up a slow-day text and email play that runs itself.

The goal is a play you build once and let run. It looks like this: the system watches your tee sheet, and when a target block drops below a threshold you set, it automatically reaches the matching segment — retirees for the Wednesday morning, twilight players for the Thursday evening — with a short text and a backup email. No blanket discount, just "there's room Wednesday morning, want it?" to the golfers most likely to say yes.

Set it up once and it becomes a standing safety net for your soft blocks. That's the difference between chasing empty tee times every week and having a system that quietly keeps them full — the same modular approach that runs across every use case we build for, from the solo owner to the multi-course operator.
