golf course business.

The 6-Step Roadmap

From idea to your first paying round.

01

Licensing, certification & insurance

Requirements vary by state and municipality, so confirm locally. Most operations need a general business license, food-service and liquor licensing if you’ll run F&B or a bar, and the right insurance before you open a gate — general liability first, with property, workers’ comp, and liquor liability added as needed. Budget several months for a liquor license.

02

Registering the business

Set up the legal foundation: choose an entity (an LLC is common for liability protection), register with your state, get an EIN from the IRS, and pull any local permits — building, health, and any zoning or commercial-recreation approvals, which can take months.

03

Tools, equipment & realistic startup costs

This is where golf ranges widest. A new build — land, irrigation, agronomy, clubhouse, cart fleet — runs into serious capital. Acquiring an existing course is more predictable; a re-launch is often the leanest path. The smart early money goes to what generates rounds from day one: booking, phone coverage, and getting found. Build your numbers from real local quotes.

04

Setting prices & quoting first rounds

Set green fees, cart fees, twilight and midweek rates, and any membership or league pricing against your local market. Quote outings and events with a clear deposit and contract from the start. Dynamic pricing keeps utilization high without eroding your brand — start simple and refine as you see demand.

05

Getting your first customers

Your first rounds come from being found and being trusted locally. Claim and optimize your Google Business Profile, get reviews early and often, run a grand-opening event and a league or two, get online booking live on day one, and market locally with email, text, and targeted ads.

06

The systems that keep you from drowning

In the first season you’re doing ten jobs. The operators who don’t burn out let systems handle the repetitive ones — answering the phone, confirming tee times, chasing leads, asking for reviews — so their hours go to the course and the golfers. Start with one connected system instead of five apps.
